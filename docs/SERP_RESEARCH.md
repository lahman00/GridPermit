# SERP Research — Solar Permit Query Landscape

Written 2026-08-15. Three representative query samples across the priority query classes (`solar permit [city]`, `[city] solar permit requirements`, `[state] solar permits`) — not hundreds, per the mission's explicit "sample, don't exhaustively scrape" instruction. Results reflect a single search session and are not treated as a stable, universal ranking (personalization/localization can shift results for any real user).

## Sample 1 — "solar permit Phoenix AZ requirements"

Ranking pages: `phoenix.gov` (official PDD SolarAPP+ page), `azsolarcenter.org` (a nonprofit solar-advocacy explainer), `phoenix.municipal.codes` (raw code text), `phoenix.gov` again (Fire Dept. permit PDF), `propertyzoned.com` (a generic real-estate/permit-lookup aggregator), `solarpermitsolutions.com` (a solar-installer-adjacent blog), `ptoedge.com` (a permit-expediting SaaS company's blog).

## Sample 2 — "Boston solar permit requirements"

Ranking pages: `greenlancer.com` (solar-industry document/permitting SaaS blog), `bostonsolar.us` (a Boston-area solar installer's own blog, four separate URLs ranking), `boston.gov` (the official city page), `meliasolar.com` (another local installer's blog), `mass.gov` (a state case-study PDF).

## Sample 3 — "California solar permits"

Ranking pages: `lci.ca.gov` (the state's own official 250-page Solar Permitting Guidebook PDF), `cityoflamesa.gov` (one city's own permit page), `nrgcleanpower.com`, `solarestique.com`, `solarpermitsolutions.com`, `ptoedge.com` — the latter four all solar-industry lead-gen/SaaS blogs.

## Pattern observed

Every sample produces the same two-tier result mix:

1. **Official government pages** (city/county/state sites, code repositories, PDFs) — authoritative but often hard to navigate, written for compliance rather than for a homeowner, and inconsistent city to city.
2. **Solar-installer and permit-SaaS blogs** (bostonsolar.us, greenlancer.com, ptoedge.com, solarpermitsolutions.com, meliasolar.com, nrgcleanpower.com) — readable, but each one exists primarily to fuel that company's own quote-request or software-signup funnel. Content is a means to a lead, not an end in itself.

**GridPermit did not appear in any of the three samples.** This is the expected, honest result for a newly-launched site with no backlink profile or domain history — not evidence of a content or technical defect. Domain age/authority is a real ranking factor Google weighs independently of on-page quality, and nothing in this audit can shortcut that.

## What this means for GridPermit's actual differentiation

The competitive field is genuinely thin on GridPermit's specific claim: **source-linked, dated, confidence-scored facts with no installer-lead-gen agenda attached to the content itself.** None of the ranking installer/SaaS blogs disclose their commercial motive as directly as GridPermit's own methodology page does, and none link every individual fact to its originating government source the way GridPermit's locality guides do. This is a real, defensible positioning gap — not a reason to copy competitor structure or content.

## What this does NOT justify

- Copying any competitor's page structure, headings, or content.
- Adding content to chase these specific SERPs before GridPermit has any ranking signal to build on.
- Treating "GridPermit isn't ranking yet" as a technical problem to fix tonight — see the Phase 21/28 findings on indexation vs. ranking vs. traffic maturity.
