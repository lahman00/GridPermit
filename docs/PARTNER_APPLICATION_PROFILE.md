## Standard application-question answers

**"How will you promote us?"**
> Through a contextual placement on the relevant solar/battery permit guide page(s) where a reader has just finished reviewing their city's actual permit requirements — the natural next step for a reader at that point is "who do I actually work with for this." No paid promotion, no email blasts, no social posts — placement is purely on-page and purely organic-search-driven.

**"Describe your audience."**
> US homeowners who are actively researching solar or battery installation permitting for their own property — not general solar shoppers, but readers who have already engaged with city-specific permit requirements (fees, inspection steps, required documents), indicating real, near-term project intent.

**"What is your traffic source?"**
> 100% organic search. GridPermit runs no paid advertising, no email list, and has no social media presence — every visitor arrives by searching for their city's solar/battery permit information.

**"Do you use paid search?"**
> No.

**"What are your monthly visitors?"**
> We don't have a verified traffic figure to share yet — our current measurement setup only covers Search Console impression/click data, not full session/visitor analytics. We'd rather say that honestly than estimate.

**"What categories/verticals do you cover?"**
> Residential solar permitting and battery/backup-power permitting — that's the entire scope of GridPermit's content today.

**"Why should we approve you?"**
> Our reader is at a specific, high-intent moment: they've just read exactly what their city requires to permit a solar or battery installation, and the natural next question is who actually does this work for them. That's a narrower, more qualified moment than general solar-shopping content, even though our absolute traffic volume is still building.

## What NOT to do with this file

- Do not add a legal entity name, business address, phone number, or traffic figure without a specific, dated, verifiable source next to it.
- Do not round up "no social media presence" to "growing social presence" or similar — state the honest zero.
- Do not let an application's optimism bleed into this file; if a real number becomes available, add it with its source, replacing the placeholder — don't just make the placeholder sound better.
