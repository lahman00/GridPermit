If Long Beach rejects a residential solar or battery permit and tells you to provide professional plans, the first question is **not** “Which plan company should I hire?”

The first question is:

**Was the project eligible for Long Beach's Express PV/ESS route, and if not, exactly which requirement moved it into full plan review?**

That distinction matters because the City currently has a streamlined route for qualifying residential rooftop PV and energy-storage projects, while more complex projects can require additional review and professional construction documents.

## What Long Beach currently allows through the Express route

The City's current Online Permitting page says the simplified online PV/ESS option is limited to simple/minor projects on **one- and two-family dwellings and accessory buildings or structures**, with PV and ESS within the stated **38.4 kW / 38.4 kWh** limits. See [City of Long Beach Online Permitting](https://www.longbeach.gov/lbcd/building/permit-center/online/).

The City's **FORM-016 Express Checklist** says the residential checklist applies to qualifying **flush rooftop-mounted PV up to 38.4 kW** on wood-framed one- and two-family dwellings and may include a residential **ESS up to 38.4 kWh**. If the project meets all checklist criteria, the City says detailed engineering analysis or formal plan review may be avoided. See [FORM-016](https://longbeach.gov/globalassets/lbcd/media-library/documents/formsapplications/form/form-016).

The City's Solar PV Process page similarly says a rooftop system that meets **IB-023 and the checklist** can avoid detailed engineering analysis and formal plan review. See [Long Beach Solar Photovoltaic Process](https://www.longbeach.gov/lbcd/building/permit-center/solar-permit/).

## Express does not mean “no plans”

Long Beach's **IB-023** still requires a complete set of electrical plans for the express path. The City lists items including:

- main service / utility disconnect locations;
- module and string counts;
- inverter/combiner make and model;
- a single-line diagram;
- grounding/bonding information;
- conductor and conduit details.

See [IB-023](https://www.longbeach.gov/globalassets/lbcd/media-library/documents/building--safety/information-bulletins/ib-023).

So a permit can be “express” and still require a technically complete drawing package.

## What can push a project out of the Express path?

Do not guess from one rejection email. Check the project against the **current FORM-016 checklist** and ask the reviewer which specific criterion was not satisfied.

Examples of facts that can change the route include:

- rooftop vs ground-mounted array;
- one-/two-family vs another building type;
- combined new + existing PV size;
- ESS size and configuration;
- historic-property status;
- line-side/source connection method;
- whether the electrical configuration satisfies the checklist;
- scope beyond the Express checklist.

The exact reason should come from the correction notice or City reviewer, not from a forum anecdote.

## Does Long Beach require a civil engineer stamp?

**GridPermit cannot confirm that every rejected residential solar/ESS project in Long Beach requires a civil-engineer stamp.**

If the City has moved the project to full plan review, the required licensed design professional can depend on the actual scope and the sheets/calculations being reviewed. Ask the reviewer to identify the required discipline rather than paying for the wrong professional.

Use these exact questions:

1. **Which FORM-016 / IB-023 criterion made this project ineligible for the Express route?**
2. **Which drawings or calculations are deficient?**
3. **Which sheets/calculations must be signed or stamped?**
4. **What California professional license/discipline is required for those documents?**
5. **Can the existing application be revised, or is a new submittal required?**
6. **Does the correction affect the Southern California Edison interconnection package too?**

Long Beach's Solar PV Process page provides Permit Center contact information and a named contact for questions about an existing solar application.

## Facts to collect before hiring a plan provider

Have these ready:

- exact City correction notice;
- project address / AHJ confirmation;
- rooftop or ground mount;
- new and existing PV kW;
- battery kWh and battery/inverter models;
- existing solar or storage on site;
- MPU/main-breaker/service changes;
- line-side/GMA/load-side interconnection method;
- current plan set and SLD;
- SCE application/interconnection status;
- whether the City explicitly named a required professional discipline.

With those facts, you can shop for the **specific missing design service** instead of buying a generic “permit plan package” and hoping it satisfies the correction.
