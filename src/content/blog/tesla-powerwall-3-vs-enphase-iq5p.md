Tesla Powerwall 3 and Enphase IQ Battery 5P are both home-energy storage systems, but they are built around different system architectures. Enphase's current 5-kWh residential battery is the IQ Battery 5P, so a "Tesla Powerwall vs. Enphase" comparison in practice means Powerwall 3 vs. one or more IQ Battery 5P units. A useful comparison starts with verified specifications and the design of the home, not a universal return-on-investment claim.

## Powerwall 3 vs. Enphase 5P: Quick Answer

A single Powerwall 3 is the larger unit: Tesla publishes 13.5 kWh of energy capacity and up to 11.5 kW continuous on-grid power for the North American configuration. One IQ Battery 5P provides 5.0 kWh of usable capacity and 3.84 kVA continuous output, so a one-to-one comparison is not energy-equivalent.

Powerwall 3 can be a more direct fit when a project benefits from a larger single battery, high continuous output, and Tesla's integrated solar-inverter architecture. IQ Battery 5P can be a more natural fit when the design favors smaller modular increments, AC coupling, lithium iron phosphate chemistry, or an existing/planned Enphase ecosystem. Neither point determines installed cost or ROI by itself.

## Where Powerwall 3 May Fit Better

Powerwall 3 combines a larger 13.5-kWh battery with high continuous output and an integrated solar inverter. That can simplify the design of a new Tesla-centered solar-plus-storage installation and can support larger simultaneous backup loads when the home's electrical service, backup equipment, and load calculations allow it.

The integrated architecture is not automatically better for every retrofit. An installer must evaluate compatibility with existing solar equipment, service-panel configuration, backup gateway or switch, utility requirements, and the loads the homeowner expects to support.

## Where IQ Battery 5P May Fit Better

The IQ Battery 5P is a 5-kWh modular, AC-coupled system. Each North American unit uses six embedded grid-forming microinverters and is rated at 3.84 kVA continuous output. Multiple units can be combined to match the required energy and power. Its embedded microinverter architecture can be a natural fit for an existing or planned Enphase ecosystem and allows storage capacity to be built in smaller increments.

A 15-year limited warranty is longer than Tesla's published 10-year term, but warranty length alone does not establish lower lifetime cost. Compare cycle limits, retained-capacity terms, labor coverage, transferability, exclusions, and the installer or manufacturer service process.

## What Actually Determines Installed Value

A defensible comparison should include:

1. **Backup-load calculation.** List the circuits and motor loads that must operate during an outage, including startup power.
2. **Usable-energy requirement.** Model how many kilowatt-hours the home needs during the desired backup period.
3. **Existing solar architecture.** Identify the current inverter, microinverters, service equipment, and interconnection arrangement.
4. **Battery dispatch under the current tariff.** Use hourly household load, solar production, import rates, and export-credit values.
5. **Complete installed cost.** Include equipment, gateways/controllers, service-panel work, permitting, interconnection, labor, and financing.
6. **Warranty and service terms.** Review the exact warranty documents and who is responsible for labor and replacement work.
7. **Permitting and placement.** Confirm local fire, building, electrical, setback, and equipment-location requirements before selecting a design.

## Why a Universal ROI Verdict Is Not Reliable

Installed prices differ by system size, electrical work, installer, financing, utility territory, and local permit requirements. Rate plans and export credits also change. A claim that one battery always has the lowest cost per kWh or the best return is not supportable without comparable current quotes and an hourly model for the specific home.

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit also changed after this article was first published. The IRS currently states that the residential credit is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Do not build a 2026 estimate around an automatic 30% credit; review the [current IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit) for the project's actual placed-in-service date.
