If you are getting quotes for a solar permitting service in **El Cerrito, California**, do not compare only the total price.

First separate the project into two approval tracks:

1. **City of El Cerrito building/electrical permitting**
2. **PG&E interconnection and Permission to Operate (PTO)**

A quote can look comprehensive while covering only one of those tracks.

## First question: can the project use El Cerrito's SolarAPP+ route?

El Cerrito currently offers automated SolarAPP+ review for qualifying residential rooftop PV and combined PV/energy-storage projects. The City's published criteria include single-family residential, roof-mounted, new systems rather than upgrades/additions, PV and ESS at or below 38.4 kW, and specified service limits.

The City also states that its SolarAPP+ path is **limited to licensed contractors**. An owner-builder or DIY applicant should not assume this automated route is available.

See the City's current [Solar System Streamlined Permitting page](https://www.el-cerrito.org/SolarPermits).
## If SolarAPP+ does not apply

The conventional permit path becomes the relevant City track. El Cerrito's Building Permits page says building permits are required for electrical work and specifically directs **Solar Projects, electrical upgrades associated with solar permits, and new Powerwalls** to its solar-permit coordination channel.

See [El Cerrito Building Permits and Applications](https://www.el-cerrito.org/buildingpermits).

Do not infer from "conventional review" that every project automatically needs the same engineering stamp. The exact structural or electrical professional requirement depends on the project and the City's correction/submittal requirements.

Before paying a plan provider, ask the City or reviewer:

- Which permit path applies to this exact project?
- Which drawings/calculations are required?
- Is a structural calculation or stamp required for this roof/site?
- Which professional discipline must sign or stamp each required item?
- Are revisions included under the existing application or treated as a new submittal?

## PG&E is a separate package

PG&E's current solar guidance says the PTO process includes an **Interconnection Application, single-line diagram (SLD), and final building permit**. PG&E separately performs its interconnection/engineering review before issuing PTO.

PG&E also publishes standard SLDs for several common Rule 21 configurations, so a custom SLD should not be assumed necessary until the exact system configuration is checked against the current application path.

See [PG&E Getting Started with Solar](https://www.pge.com/en/clean-energy/solar/getting-started-with-solar.html) and [PG&E Interconnections & Renewables](https://www.pge.com/en/about/doing-business-with-pge/interconnections/interconnections-and-renewables.html).
## What a permitting-service quote should itemize

Ask the provider to price these separately instead of selling an undefined "permit package":

- City/AHJ plan set preparation;
- roof/site plan;
- electrical SLD;
- structural calculations and stamp **only if required**;
- equipment/spec-sheet assembly;
- City application/submittal support;
- correction/revision rounds;
- PG&E interconnection application support;
- PG&E correction/revision support;
- final-document/PTO handoff.

Also ask what is **not** included: City fees, SolarAPP+ fees, engineering stamps, PG&E fees, inspection coordination and resubmittals can be separate.

A **$1,200 quote cannot be judged from price alone**. It could be excessive for a simple standardized project or reasonable for a package that includes real engineering, stamps and multiple revision cycles. GridPermit cannot confirm fair market pricing without the project scope and deliverables.

## Facts to collect before accepting a quote

Provide the provider with: rooftop vs ground mount; PV kW; battery kWh; inverter/PCS models; existing PV/storage; owner-builder vs licensed contractor; MPU/service change; point of interconnection; current City correction notice; and PG&E application status.

That lets you compare providers on the **actual missing work**, not on a generic package name.
