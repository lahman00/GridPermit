# SDG&E Electricity Rates & NEM 3.0 Analysis: The Complete 2026 Guide

> **FTC Disclosure:** GridPermit is an independent resource funded by lead referrals. When you request installer quotes through our tools, we may receive compensation at no cost to you.

San Diego Gas & Electric (**SDG&E**) holds the unenviable title of offering the most expensive residential electricity rates in the continental United States. With summer peak rates frequently climbing above **$0.64 per kilowatt-hour (kWh)**, homeowners in San Diego County face unprecedented monthly utility bills.

Following the CPUC implementation of **NEM 3.0 (Net Billing Tariff)**, traditional solar installations that send power back to the grid during the day no longer offer rapid payback. This exhaustive guide breaks down the exact mechanics of SDG&E tariffs, battery integration payback models, and tax credit strategies required to neutralize San Diego utility costs in 2026.

---

## Part 1: Why SDG&E Utility Rates Are Skyrocketing

SDG&E’s rate structure is driven by three main operational factors:
1. **Wildfire Mitigation Infrastructure:** Massive capital expenditure allocated to undergrounding power lines in high-fire threat districts (HFTD).
2. **Transmission & Distribution (T&D) Overhead:** Fixed delivery fees embedded into per-kWh consumption rates.
3. **Time-of-Use (TOU) Rate Structures:** Aggressive rate spikes during peak evening hours when solar generation drops off.

### Current SDG&E TOU-DR1 Rate Breakdown (Summer 2026)

| Time Window | Tariff Period | Average Cost per kWh |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **4:00 PM – 9:00 PM** | **On-Peak** | **$0.62 – $0.68 / kWh** |
| **6:00 AM – 4:00 PM** | **Off-Peak** | **$0.42 – $0.48 / kWh** |
| **Midnight – 6:00 AM** | **Super Off-Peak** | **$0.24 – $0.28 / kWh** |

## Part 2: The NEM 3.0 Tariff Reality for San Diego

Under legacy NEM 2.0 rules, SDG&E credited homeowners 1-to-1 for every kWh of solar energy sent to the grid. Under NEM 3.0, the **Avoided Cost Calculator (ACC)** determines export credits.

During sunny daytime hours (10 AM to 2 PM), when California's grid is flooded with solar energy, SDG&E credits exports at approximately **$0.05 to $0.08 per kWh**—an **85% drop** compared to peak retail charges.
