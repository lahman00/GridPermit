On SDG&E's Solar Billing Plan, electricity you buy from the grid and surplus solar you send back are priced separately. Before accepting a savings estimate, identify your import rate, the export-pricing table that applies to your system, and the company supplying your generation service.

This guide helps you find those records and compare a solar or battery proposal. Jump to [your rates and data](#find-your-actual-rates-and-data), [export credits](#use-the-export-table-that-applies-to-your-system), [battery checks](#check-the-assumptions-in-a-battery-quote), or [organizing quotes](#keep-your-quotes-and-source-notes-together).

## First confirm which solar plan you have

The Solar Billing Plan is SDG&E's name for the Net Billing Tariff (NBT), often called NEM 3.0. The [CPUC's Net Billing overview](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/NEM/) describes the change for qualifying interconnection applications from April 15, 2023 and identifies EV-TOU-5 as SDG&E's residential NBT rate.

Check the plan shown on your bill. Existing NEM customers and commercial accounts should not assume that the residential Solar Billing Plan rules apply to them. A proposal should name the plan it models and explain any proposed change.

Under NBT, solar used in your home offsets purchased energy. Exported energy earns credits based on its value to the grid, usually below retail prices. A yearly total of solar production does not show how much you use directly, export or buy later. Those quantities need separate treatment.

## Find your actual rates and data

Start with your current bill and the official resources below. Save the rate schedule, effective date and source link used for each estimate.

| What to establish | Where to find it | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Import rate | [SDG&E Total Electric Rates](https://www.sdge.com/total-electric-rates) | Match the schedule and effective dates to your bill, including any applicable CARE or Medical Baseline variant. |
| Generation provider | Your bill and [SDG&E's CCA explanation](https://www.sdge.com/solar/solar-billing-plan) | If a Community Choice Aggregator supplies generation, obtain its generation import prices and export credits separately. |
| Export-credit pricing | [SDG&E export-pricing files](https://www.sdge.com/solar/solar-billing-plan/export-pricing) | Confirm the applicable export plan/year, billing period and generation/delivery components. |
| Interval electricity data | [Green Button download instructions](https://www.sdge.com/green-button/download-my-green-button-data) | Choose the electric meter, date range and format; check for gaps before using the file. |

For Green Button, SDG&E's instructions take you through **Usage → Electric meter → Green Button Download** in My Energy Center. Gather a full year if available so the comparison includes seasonal changes. Keep bills, account details and downloaded records in your own files; do not upload them to GridPermit.

### If SD Community Power or Clean Energy Alliance supplies your electricity

SDG&E says a CCA sets its customers' generation import charges and generation export credits. Use [SD Community Power's residential rates](https://sdcommunitypower.org/residential-rates/) or [Clean Energy Alliance's Solar Impact information](https://thecleanenergyalliance.org/solar-impact/), as applicable. Do not substitute SDG&E's generation component for your CCA's, or add a bundled total to delivery charges a second time.

## Use the export table that applies to your system

SDG&E's [export-pricing directory](https://www.sdge.com/solar/solar-billing-plan/export-pricing) lists both Current 2026 and Legacy 2026 files, alongside other legacy years. Do not choose a file solely because its year matches today's date. Confirm the export plan shown on your bill and ask SDG&E which file applies if it is unclear.

The pricing varies by month, type of day and hour, and separates generation from delivery. Use the [official export-file guide](https://www.sdge.com/sites/default/files/documents/Understanding%20Solar%20Billing%20Plan%20Export%20Pricing%20Web.pdf?nid=26486) to read the fields. A single average cents-per-kWh value can hide when your system actually exports.

For an existing system, SDG&E also directs customers to [My Energy Center](https://myenergycenter.com/) for the billing-period **Export Data and Pricing** spreadsheet. Compare a representative bill with those records before treating a model's export-credit estimate as reliable. CCA customers still need their provider's generation pricing.

## Account for the Base Services Charge once

SDG&E's [Base Services Charge explanation](https://www.sdge.com/electric-billing) says this charge replaces EV-TOU-5's former fixed monthly service fee and replaces minimum bills. Do not add all three to a current estimate.

Solar customers still pay the Base Services Charge. SDG&E's [Solar Billing Plan guidance](https://www.sdge.com/solar/solar-billing-plan) says export credits cannot offset it. Keep this and other charges that export credits cannot offset separate from energy savings; a high production estimate does not establish a zero-dollar bill.

## Check the assumptions in a battery quote

A battery can shift solar energy into later household use. Whether that justifies its cost depends on your actual use, equipment, settings and prices. Ask for a comparison of solar-only and solar-plus-storage using the same household demand and solar-production assumptions.

If you already have solar, grid interval data alone is incomplete for this task. SDG&E's [smart-meter guidance](https://www.sdge.com/residential/savings-center/smart-meters/your-smart-meter/smart-meters-net-metering) explains that its meter does not show total solar production. Pair the grid data with inverter or monitoring-system production records when evaluating a battery retrofit.

Ask the proposal to show:

- How much energy serves the home immediately, charges the battery, reaches the grid and is imported later.
- Battery capacity and power output, operating losses, backup reserve and degradation assumptions.
- The import schedule and export-credit file used, plus any separate CCA generation prices.
- The full installed price, financing costs and exclusions, including permitting, interconnection and electrical upgrades.
- Bill savings separately from outage-backup benefits. A reserve kept for backup is not fully available for daily bill reduction.
- Results under less favorable assumptions, such as lower usable solar production, higher installation cost or changed household demand.

Do not count the same stored energy as both an avoided grid purchase and an export earning credit. This is a comparison check, not a promise that a particular battery size or operating schedule will pay back.

Homeowners in the **City of San Diego** whose electric utility is **SDG&E** and who already have solar can review the [battery-retrofit quote option and its eligibility check](/blog/sdge-battery-roi-guide/#battery-quote-options). This is a paid referral route, not a guaranteed saving or an offer for every SDG&E service-area city.

## Check incentives before subtracting them from a quote

Confirm current SGIP availability, eligibility and the status of any proposed reservation. An advertised program amount is not an award to your household. GridPermit's <a href="/blog/sgip-battery-rebates-california/" data-track-click="internal_resource_clicked">SGIP battery rebate guide</a> identifies program sources and questions to verify.

Do not automatically deduct a 30% residential federal credit from a new 2026 installation. The [IRS Residential Clean Energy Credit guidance](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit) states that the credit is unavailable for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. Check the project's actual dates and obtain tax advice where appropriate; a quote's assumed credit does not establish your eligibility.
